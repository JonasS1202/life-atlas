# Procedure: deep mode (`/atlas-deep`)

Only when the user asks for it. Goal: Atlas understands who the user is, beyond facts: values, motives, recurring
patterns, goals, tensions between them. The result sharpens `self/` and makes Atlas's advice fit the person.

## How

- A calm conversation, one open question at a time; follow what the user brings up. Reflect back what you hear in
  your own words and ask whether it fits. No questionnaires, no scoring.
- Useful directions: what gives energy and what drains it; decisions they are proud of and why; recurring situations
  that go wrong; what they want to be true in one, five, ten years; what they avoid; how they want Atlas to challenge
  or support them.
- Name patterns as observations with evidence from the conversation ("you mentioned twice that …"), never as labels.

## Boundaries

- No diagnoses, no clinical terms applied to the user, not a substitute for therapy or medical advice.
- If the user mentions thoughts of self-harm, acute crisis, or severe distress: stop the exploration, respond with care,
  encourage contacting someone they trust or professional help (local emergency number, crisis line), and do not
  continue the deep mode in that chat.
- The user can stop at any time; nothing is pushed.

## Keeping it

At the end, show the user in a few lines what you would keep (in their words where possible) and ask. Only what they
approve goes to `self/` (pages tagged `self` or `preference`, marked as sensitive in the summary). Nothing from deep mode
goes into the daily log or any other page without that approval: the `Atlas:` line of such a chat says only
`Atlas: deep-mode session` (plus "self/ updated" if the user approved something), because the stop hook copies that
line into the daily log.
