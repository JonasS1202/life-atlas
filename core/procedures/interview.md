# Procedure: getting to know the user (`/atlas-interview`)

Used at the end of the installation, on request, and by the rhythm in `brain/settings.md` (`questions`).

1. **Find the gaps first (facts, then depth).** Read `self/README.md`, the area READMEs, `people/`, `atlas.py check`
   (pages without summary, projects without goal) and `atlas.py deadlines`.
   - First look for missing *facts*: contracts without notice period, areas with no pages although the inventory
     suggested them, people without relation, goals without date.
   - **Never say "I already know everything":** If structural facts are already filled in, look for *thin pages* (a
     person page that only states a name/relation, a job or finance page with numbers but no context or goals, `self/`
     sections that lack depth) and ask about the story, relationship, or priorities behind those facts — or offer to
     switch directly into `/atlas-deep` (`procedures/deep.md`) for a deeper friend-and-psychologist conversation.
2. **Ask one question at a time**, short, concrete, with a reason when it helps ("I found insurance letters but nothing
   about a car. Do you have one?"). Offer an easy way out ("skip", "later", "enough").
3. **File each answer immediately** in its place (summary, tags, `due:`), not at the end.
4. At most about ten questions per round; stop earlier when the user's answers get short. Summarise in two lines what
   Atlas now knows better.
5. Rhythm questions ("evening-3": three diary questions in the evening; "weekly-session": a short round once a week):
   lighter questions about the day or the week (what happened, what is on your mind, what is next); answers go to the
   daily journal, lasting things to their pages.
6. "Stop the questions" or similar: set `questions: off` in `settings.md`, confirm in one line.
